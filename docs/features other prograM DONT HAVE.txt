Features Other Programs Almost Never Have
1. Predictive Risk Modelling (not just retrospective scoring)
Most programs only score what already happened. SafeSteps could flag trajectory, not just snapshots:

Trend-line alerts — "this domain has declined 2 consecutive periods" before it hits crisis level
Pattern matching against historical case outcomes (which combinations of indicators preceded past breakdowns)
Useful caution: this needs careful design to avoid bias — flag for human review, never auto-decide

2. Worker Wellbeing & Burnout Monitoring
Almost no program tracks this, yet worker burnout directly degrades assessment quality:

Caseload intensity tracking per worker
Self-report wellbeing check-ins for workers themselves
Automatic caseload rebalancing suggestions to admin

3. Sibling & Whole-Family View
Most systems are single-child or single-parent centric:

A family-level dashboard showing all children and how they're tracking relative to each other
Sibling relationship quality tracking (often ignored entirely)

4. Two-Way Transparency Dashboard for Parents
Parents are usually assessed on but rarely see how scoring works:

A plain-language view showing exactly which indicators feed their score and why
Builds trust and reduces the "black box" feeling that fuels disengagement and gaming

5. Cultural & Language Adaptation Built into Scoring
Most rubrics are culturally generic:

Configurable rubric variants for cultural context (e.g. different "appropriate" indicators for Aboriginal kinship care models)
Interpreter-flagged sessions automatically noted in evidence reports so scoring isn't misread as non-engagement

6. Post-Reunification Early Warning Network
Beyond Home Again's own monitoring — connecting outward:

Optional opt-in links to school, GP, and childcare so attendance/health flags surface automatically rather than only via self-report
Reduces reliance on the family to self-disclose problems

7. De-Identified Aggregate Research Output
No program in this space publishes outcome data well:

Auto-generated de-identified program effectiveness data for continuous improvement and funding/evaluation reporting
This is a major credibility differentiator for funders and courts

8. Parent-Authored Voice in the Court Report
Court reports are normally 100% professional-voice:

A structured section where the parent's own reflections (in their words) sit alongside worker scoring — shows growth in their own language, which courts and CP often find compelling

9. Digital Consent & Rights Literacy Module
Parents are told they're being assessed but rarely taught their rights within that:

A short, plain-language in-app module explaining what's collected, why, how it's used, and what rights they have to respond to or contest findings — builds legitimacy and reduces adversarial dynamics

11. Visit/Contact Logistics Layer
Almost no program's app actually handles the logistics of contact visits themselves:

In-app scheduling for supervised/unsupervised contact with calendar sync for parent, worker, carer, and child
Carer-side check-in (foster/kinship carer notes on child's state before/after contact) — currently a totally missing data source in most systems
Automatic handover logs (who dropped off, picked up, any incidents) timestamped and GPS-tagged

12. Foster/Kinship Carer Portal — A Fifth User Type
Carers are usually invisible to the system even though they hold huge amounts of relevant data:

Carer daily/weekly observations on the child (sleep, mood, behaviour after contact)
Carer-worker messaging
Carer wellbeing/burnout check-ins (carer breakdown is a major unplanned disruption risk)

13. Legal/Court Timeline Integration
Court dates drive everything but are rarely embedded in the system itself:

Court date tracker with automatic countdown and report-deadline alerts to workers
Version-locked snapshot of all evidence "as at" each court date (so reports can't be retroactively altered post-hearing — critical for legal defensibility)
Lawyer/legal guardian read-only access tier

14. Multi-Agency Information Sharing Layer
Most systems are silos; real cases involve many agencies:

Structured, permissioned data-sharing exports formatted for police, health, education, and other CP-linked agencies (rather than ad hoc emails/PDFs)
Consent-tracked sharing — who shared what with whom, when, under what authority

15. Relapse/Crisis Re-Entry Pathway
Most programs treat exit as final; real life isn't linear:

A defined "step back in" workflow if a family needs to re-enter Reunification or Home Again after closure, preserving historical data rather than starting a blank new case
Clear criteria and lighter-touch re-assessment rather than a full restart

16. Child Participation in Decision-Making (not just check-ins)
Most "child's voice" features are passive reporting. True participation goes further:

Age-appropriate tools for the child to contribute to their own case plan goals
Child feedback specifically on whether they feel decisions are being explained to them

17. Bias & Equity Auditing on the Scoring Data Itself
Almost no program checks whether its own tool is fair:

Periodic aggregate analysis: do certain cultural groups, genders, or disability statuses score systematically lower on subjective rubric items, independent of outcomes?
Flags to admin/program designers, not auto-correction — human review of potential rubric bias

18. Worker Decision Rationale Capture
Court and oversight bodies increasingly expect this:

Mandatory short rationale field whenever a worker's score deviates significantly from automated/historical pattern — creates an audit trail of professional judgment, not just numbers

19. Family Strengths Repository (separate from risk)
Strengths usually get buried inside risk-framed rubrics:

A standalone, continuously updated "strengths and protective factors" record that's structurally equal in visibility to the risk log — so reports don't read as purely deficit-based

20. Independent Advocate/Support Person Access
Parents in adversarial systems benefit from independent support:

Optional read-only or limited access tier for a parent's chosen independent advocate or support person (not a worker, not CP) — common in best-practice family law contexts, rare in CP tech

As domains sustain high scores, the app reduces submission burden automatically (e.g. daily check-ins drop to every second day) — visible, motivating, and reduces surveillance fatigue without a worker manually changing settings


Most of these aren't curriculum — they're system design choices that address trust, sustainability, and fairness, which is exactly where most reunification programs are weakest.
