# SafeSteps Accessibility and Multilingual Roadmap v1.0

SafeSteps targets meaningful access, not literal translation. Normal support uses short, concrete, grade 6–8 language; crisis content is shorter. Critical screens must retain a prominent emergency action, one question at a time, large controls, predictable focus, screen-reader announcements, high contrast, reduced motion and an obvious quick exit.

## Delivery phases

1. English baseline: keyboard and screen-reader operation, 200% scaling, high contrast, low-stimulation crisis layouts and simplified wording.
2. Human-reviewed Arabic, Spanish, Vietnamese, Simplified/Traditional Chinese and Hindi; then Punjabi, Farsi/Dari according to service need.
3. Community-reviewed cultural adaptation and jurisdiction-specific referrals. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander language support must be developed with local partners, not raw machine translation.

## Publishing governance

Machine translation may create drafts only. Every locale needs a named language owner, safeguarding review, UI overflow/RTL testing where relevant, and representative user review. Crisis, domestic-violence, child-protection, self-harm, mandatory-reporting and referral wording is Tier 3: it also requires domain-specialist approval, versioning, regression tests and verified local contacts before activation.

Track selection and completion by locale, translation-error reports, accessibility complaints, screen-reader issues, crisis-action use and referral failures. Metrics must be de-identified and must not create dangerous browsing or disclosure histories.
