# SafeSteps Assessment Fairness And Reviewability Framework

Last updated: 2026-07-16

SafeSteps assessments must remain structured decision support. They must not convert worker opinion, unverified allegations, AI output, poverty, disability, distress, disagreement, or historical concerns into adverse findings without reliable evidence, child-impact analysis, context, proportionality review, and authorised human oversight.

## Core Rules

- Child safety and rights are central.
- Disagreement with a worker is not lack of insight.
- Poverty is not neglect.
- Disability is not incapacity.
- Being subjected to domestic violence is not the same as causing it.
- Distress during assessment is not proof of instability.
- Historical risk is not automatically current risk.
- Missing information is not negative evidence.
- Serious conclusions require reliable, corroborated evidence.
- Families must have a meaningful opportunity to respond, correct errors, and request review.
- Intervention must be the least restrictive response capable of protecting the child.

## Facts Before Findings

Assessment workflow should require this order:

1. Record the event or concern.
2. Identify the original source.
3. Separate observation from interpretation.
4. Add parent and child accounts where safe.
5. Upload supporting and contrary evidence.
6. Examine context, control, barriers, and available alternatives.
7. Consider plausible alternative explanations.
8. Make a finding only after the evidence record is visible.

## Evidence Reliability

Every item should be classified before it affects scoring or findings:

| Level | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| A - Verified | Original document, recording, direct observation, or reliable independent record |
| B - Corroborated | Supported by two or more sufficiently independent sources |
| C - Unverified | Single account not independently confirmed |
| D - Opinion | Professional interpretation rather than observed fact |
| E - Disputed | Conflicting accounts or contradictory evidence |
| F - Incorrect or outdated | Demonstrably wrong, superseded, or no longer current |

Repeated retelling of one original allegation should not count as independent corroboration.

## Required Finding Chain

An adverse finding should identify:

- Exact behaviour.
- Date, setting, duration, and frequency.
- Evidence source and reliability.
- Child impact or reasonably foreseeable child impact.
- Parent explanation.
- Alternative explanations considered.
- Parent control or constrained choice.
- Supports offered and whether they were accessible.
- Protective actions taken.
- Current relevance and evidence expiry.
- Less restrictive responses considered.
- Reviewer approval where the finding is serious or disputed.

If a required link is missing, the system should label the matter unresolved, support need, or insufficient evidence rather than parental failure.

## Responsibility Allocation

For each event, SafeSteps should consider whether responsibility or contribution sits with:

- Parent or caregiver.
- Other parent or household member.
- Person causing domestic violence.
- Child-development or disability-related needs.
- Government department.
- Service provider.
- Housing, financial, transport, or service-access systems.
- Unpredictable external event.
- Unknown or insufficient evidence.

This prevents assigning all responsibility to the assessed parent by default.

## Professional And Service-System Review

For each parent requirement, record whether:

- It was clearly explained in writing.
- It was specific, achievable, and connected to the identified risk.
- Enough time was provided.
- The required service existed, accepted the referral, and was suitable.
- It was affordable, accessible, culturally safe, and disability-adjusted.
- Worker turnover, missed appointments, delayed referrals, or changed expectations affected progress.
- The combined workload was realistic alongside court, contact, employment, school, childcare, medical, and transport demands.

A parent should not be marked non-compliant where the system did not provide a reasonable pathway to compliance.

## Bias, Circular Reasoning, And Moving Goalposts

SafeSteps should trigger review when:

- Disagreement, complaints, or requests for evidence are treated as non-compliance.
- Subjective labels replace behaviour-specific findings.
- Positive evidence is omitted or ignored.
- New requirements appear without new evidence.
- Completed requirements are later treated as incomplete.
- Several scores rely on the same underlying event.
- A complaint is treated as evidence of hostility.
- Distress caused by removal is treated as evidence justifying removal.
- Limited contact prevents skill demonstration, then the absence of demonstration is used to continue limited contact.

## Proportionality

Before recommending restriction, the record should answer:

- What specific harm is being prevented?
- How likely and serious is that harm?
- What current evidence supports the prediction?
- Is there a less restrictive way to manage it?
- What harm could the intervention itself cause?
- Does the expected safety benefit outweigh attachment, cultural, sibling, school, and identity disruption?
- When must the decision be reviewed?

## Child Voice Protection

Child voice records should separately preserve:

- The child's words where safe.
- Who asked questions.
- Where and when the conversation occurred.
- Who was present.
- Whether questions were open or leading.
- Age, communication needs, and supports.
- Consistency without repeated prompting.
- What the child wants and why.
- Whether sharing the information may create risk.

Children should not be pressured to protect an adult, please a worker, earn rewards, or avoid consequences by giving a particular answer.

## Reviewability And Access

Everything relevant to a decision should remain available for authorised review:

- Original assessments and reports.
- Evidence relied upon and contrary evidence.
- Parent and child responses.
- Allegations and original sources.
- Corrections, disputes, and review outcomes.
- Case-plan versions.
- Scores and scoring rules.
- AI outputs, source material, confidence warnings, and reviewer notes.
- Worker notes that influenced decisions.
- Approvals and independent reviews.
- Dates, times, identities, and audit history for material actions.

Reviewable does not mean editable by everyone. Access must be role-based, least-privilege, logged, time-limited where appropriate, and protective of child safety, privacy, legal restrictions, and third-party confidentiality.

Corrections should preserve history:

```text
Original entry -> correction submitted -> evidence reviewed -> finding amended -> affected scores recalculated -> recipients notified
```

The inaccurate version remains in restricted audit history, clearly marked incorrect, superseded, or disputed, and must not continue influencing current scoring.

## AI Limits

AI may organise records, identify contradictions, flag subjective language, suggest alternative explanations, and check whether fairness questions were completed. AI must not independently decide whether abuse occurred, whether a parent is credible, whether contact should be restricted, whether reunification should occur, or whether someone lacks insight.

Every AI-generated statement should show sources, confidence, limitations, and the responsible human reviewer.

## Finalisation Checkpoint

Before a serious recommendation is released, the reviewer should be able to truthfully state:

> I have identified the specific current risk, assessed the reliability and limitations of the evidence, considered the child's and parent's accounts, separated disadvantage from neglect, examined disability and cultural factors, identified what was within the parent's reasonable control, reviewed service-system contributions, considered protective evidence, and tested less restrictive alternatives. I can explain why this recommendation is necessary and proportionate.

If this cannot be completed, the recommendation should not proceed without further evidence or independent review.
