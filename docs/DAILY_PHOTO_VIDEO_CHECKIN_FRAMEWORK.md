# Daily Photo and Video Check-In Framework

This framework supports daily evidence of home safety, basic care conditions, and parent stability. It should be used proportionately and with clear privacy, consent, storage, and child-safety rules.

The purpose is to identify longitudinal patterns, not to punish ordinary mess, poverty, or one-off hard days. Concerns should be interpreted with context and cross-checked against home visits, worker observations, and parent explanations.

## Why The System Is Useful

| Feature | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Camera-only uploads | Reduces use of old or staged photos from the library |
| Timestamps | Shows when the evidence was created |
| Daily requirement | Builds a longitudinal evidence record |
| Multiple areas required | Reduces selective disclosure of only the best area |
| Repeated pattern tracking | Shows whether home safety is sustained, improving, or deteriorating |

## Daily Submission Areas

### Living Environment

| Area | What should be visible |
| --- | --- |
| Kitchen | Bench surfaces, sink, food storage, fridge contents where appropriate |
| Bathroom | Cleanliness, soap, toilet paper, towels, hygiene supplies |
| Child's bedroom | Bed, clothing access, age-appropriate items, child-ready setup |
| Living area | Hazard-free, functional, safe movement space |
| Outdoor areas | Safe, maintained, no obvious hazards |
| Entry and exits | Clear, secure, usable in emergency |

### Personal Hygiene Check-In

| Area | What it may show |
| --- | --- |
| Parent selfie | Grooming, clothing, alertness, readiness for daily parenting tasks |
| Consistent timing | Morning or agreed check-in window to show routine |
| Notes field | Context for illness, work, appointments, cultural needs, or unusual circumstances |

## Daily Checklist Rubric

Use a simple 1-3 score for each daily area.

| Area | Score 1 | Score 2 | Score 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kitchen | Unsafe, dirty, no usable food/preparation space | Adequate but needs improvement | Clean enough, functional, stocked |
| Bathroom | Unsafe, dirty, hygiene supplies absent | Adequate and usable | Clean, functional, supplies present |
| Child's room | Unsafe, inadequate, or not child-ready | Adequate but basic | Safe, nurturing, child-ready |
| Living area | Hazards present or unsafe | Adequate and usable | Safe, functional, child-aware |
| Outdoor area | Unsafe hazards or poor access | Adequate | Safe and maintained |
| Entry/exits | Blocked, insecure, or unsafe | Usable with minor concerns | Clear, secure, emergency-ready |
| Personal hygiene | Concerning presentation or alertness | Adequate | Well-maintained and alert |

## Daily Score Interpretation

| Average score | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| 1.0-1.5 | High concern; requires review, support, or safety action |
| 1.6-2.4 | Mixed or adequate; monitor patterns and support improvement |
| 2.5-3.0 | Stable and protective pattern demonstrated |

Do not rely only on an average. A serious safety issue in one area can override an otherwise adequate daily score.

## Anti-Gaming Layers

| Risk | Counter-measure |
| --- | --- |
| Old or staged images | Camera-only upload and timestamp validation |
| Cleaning one area at a fixed time | Random or varied submission windows where policy permits |
| Selective room disclosure | Required multi-area checklist |
| Edited or interrupted video | Periodic single continuous video walkthrough with no cuts |
| Photos not from the approved address | GPS metadata only where consent, law, and policy permit |
| Difference between photos and reality | Worker cross-reference with home visit observations |
| Pattern drop-offs | Longitudinal flags for weekends, contact days, pay-cycle periods, or high-stress periods |

## Video Walkthrough Protocol

Use periodically, not necessarily daily.

Minimum requirements:

- Single continuous recording
- Starts at entry point
- Shows kitchen, bathroom, child's room, living area, exits, and agreed outdoor area
- No child private information, school identifiers, medication labels, or sensitive documents visible where avoidable
- Parent narrates date, time, and any context
- Worker reviews against the same safety rubric

## Privacy and Safeguarding Rules

- Explain purpose, storage, access, retention, and sharing before collecting media.
- Avoid capturing children in private, vulnerable, unclothed, distressed, or identifying situations.
- Avoid capturing third parties without consent.
- Allow contextual notes so evidence is not misread.
- Use GPS only if lawful, proportionate, consented or otherwise authorised, and documented in policy.
- Restrict access to authorised workers.
- Treat media as sensitive child and family evidence.

## Evidence Report Use

This system can show:

- Daily home safety pattern
- Food, hygiene, utilities, and child-readiness indicators
- Parent routine and presentation over time
- Whether standards improve, remain stable, or deteriorate
- Whether photo/video evidence aligns with home visits and collateral information
- Whether risks cluster around specific days, events, contact visits, or stressors
