# Reunification Program Assessment Battery

This battery supports SafeSteps reunification work by separating licensed clinical tools, worker-scored family functioning tools, program rubrics, parent-child observation, and final evidence reporting.

SafeSteps should not reproduce, administer, or score restricted psychological instruments unless the service holds the required licence and the assessor is appropriately qualified. For restricted tools, SafeSteps should store metadata, score summaries, interpretation notes, and uploaded reports.

## Core Defence Against Performed Compliance

The program should reduce the risk of coached, performative, or short-term compliance through multi-source, multi-method, longitudinal assessment. The goal is not to claim that change can never be faked; the goal is to make conclusions depend on consistent evidence across time, settings, tools, and observers.

Key evidence sources:

- Self-report tools
- Worker observation
- Third-party collateral reports
- Psychologist assessment
- Video-coded interaction
- Announced, varied, short-notice, and where lawful/policy-approved unannounced contact observations
- Inconsistency tracking over time

The core question is whether stated insight aligns with behaviour, stress responses, collateral reports, parent-child interaction, and sustained generalisation outside the program room.

## Full Program Structure

### Pre-Entry: Week 0

| Task | Tool | SafeSteps record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full psychosocial history | Structured intake interview | Intake summary, risks, strengths, family context, service history |
| Baseline personality | PAI or MCMI-IV | External report metadata and score summary |
| Attachment style | Adult Attachment Interview | External report metadata and attachment formulation |
| Trauma history | ACE structured interview + TSI-2 | Trauma history summary and external TSI-2 report if used |
| Cognitive functioning | WAIS-IV via psychologist | External report metadata and functional implications |
| Substance use baseline | AUDIT + DAST-10 + urine screening | Screener scores, screening dates, pathology/urine evidence where applicable |
| Risk assessment | SDM Risk Assessment tool | Risk level, safety concerns, strengths, recommended actions |
| Parenting attitudes baseline | AAPI-2 | External score summary and parenting-attitude domains |

### Phase 1: Weeks 1-4 - Engagement and Foundation

| Focus | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Genuine vs performative engagement | Worker behavioural rubric scored each session |
| Emotional regulation baseline | GAD-7, PHQ-9, PSI-4 |
| Insight into removal | Structured reflective interview scored for accountability vs minimisation |
| Consistency check | Compare intake interview statements to self-report responses and flag contradictions |

Anti-gaming safeguards:

- Ask the same core questions in different formats across different weeks.
- Keep a contradiction log for meaningful inconsistencies, not minor wording differences.
- Score defensiveness, blame-shifting, minimisation, and over-compliance explicitly.
- Record examples, not just global impressions.

### Phase 2: Weeks 5-10 - Skill Building and Deep Assessment

| Focus | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Parenting knowledge | Pre/post knowledge tests for each module |
| Parent-child interaction | DPICS or CARE-Index video coding |
| Emotional availability | Emotional Availability Scales |
| Stress response under pressure | Deliberately challenging but safe session tasks and scenarios |
| Collateral information | Contact with support workers, GP, housing, treatment providers, school, and other services where consent/legal authority permits |

Anti-gaming safeguards:

- Vary formats: group, individual, home visit, contact observation, written reflection, and scenario response.
- Include safe but challenging scenarios to observe authentic stress responses.
- Cross-reference the parent's description of supports with actual collateral contacts.
- Use video where lawful, consented, and policy-approved so coding is based on observable behaviour.

### Phase 3: Weeks 11-16 - Integration and Generalisation

| Focus | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Skill transfer outside sessions | Home visit observations and collateral reports |
| Sustained change vs temporary compliance | Compare Phase 1 and Phase 3 scores for stability |
| Child-focused thinking | Structured scenarios testing whether the parent centres child needs or adult needs |
| Relapse indicators | Ongoing substance screening and mental health re-assessment |
| Protective capacity | Full NCFAS-G re-administration where licensed/approved |

Anti-gaming safeguards:

- Use short-notice or unannounced visits only where lawful, ethical, and agency policy permits.
- Use scenario-based questioning because hypothetical situations reveal reasoning under pressure.
- Ask the parent to explain the child's needs, feelings, triggers, and perspective in detail.
- Re-administer baseline tools where appropriate. Genuine change should show stability across more than one evidence source.

### Phase 4: Exit Assessment - Weeks 17-18

Re-administer baseline tools where appropriate and add:

- Forensic Parenting Capacity Assessment by registered psychologist or forensic psychologist where required
- Longitudinal change report across every domain and phase
- Graphed score trajectory
- Contradiction and consistency analysis
- Collateral summary
- Parent-child interaction summary
- Court-ready evidence report

## Anti-Gaming Indicators

Add these indicators to every domain score.

| Indicator | What it checks |
| --- | --- |
| Internal consistency | Whether the parent says substantially consistent things across sessions, tools, and time |
| Behavioural-verbal alignment | Whether actions match stated beliefs and intentions |
| Stress response authenticity | How the parent responds when sessions become genuinely difficult |
| Collateral alignment | Whether external reports align with self-report |
| Generalisation evidence | Whether change appears outside sessions, not only in program settings |

Each indicator should be scored using the same 1-4 anchored scale and supported with examples.

| Score | Anti-gaming anchor |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Significant contradictions, mismatch between words and behaviour, or no evidence of generalisation |
| 2 | Some alignment, but inconsistencies remain material or require significant prompting/support |
| 3 | Mostly consistent across program settings and some external contexts |
| 4 | Consistent across time, tools, observers, stress conditions, and external contexts |

## Phase 4: Exit and Evidence Report

The exit report should compile:

- Baseline vs exit scores
- Midpoint trajectory where available
- Qualitative narrative per domain
- Risk indicators reduced, unchanged, or increased
- Protective capacities demonstrated
- Parent-child interaction evidence
- Service engagement and follow-through
- Recommendations with evidence basis

## Intensive Tiered Assessment Model

For a high-intensity reunification program, assessment evidence should be multi-source. No single score or tool should determine parenting capacity. The strongest evidence base combines parent self-report, structured worker observation, standardised psychological testing, parent-child interaction observation, and forensic formulation where required.

| Tier | Tool type | Who administers | SafeSteps role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 1 | Self-report questionnaires and program rubrics | Program worker, case worker, or trained facilitator within policy scope | Administer permitted tools, store scores, track change |
| Tier 2 | Structured observation tools | Trained program staff or supervisor | Record observations, score program checklists, upload notes |
| Tier 3 | Standardised psychological and neuropsychological tests | Registered psychologist or appropriately qualified clinician | Store completion metadata, score summaries, reports, recommendations |
| Tier 4 | Forensic parenting capacity protocols | Forensic psychologist or court-appointed expert | Store court-ready reports, limits, recommendations, and evidence basis |

Tiers 1-3 running together can provide a defensible picture of change for child protection and court contexts. Tier 4 should be used when the matter requires independent forensic opinion, contested expert evidence, or court-directed parenting capacity assessment.

## SafeSteps-Native vs External Assessment Use

SafeSteps can build and score:

- Program engagement rubric
- Reflective functioning journal rubric
- Behavioural tracking rubric
- Structured parent-child observation checklist aligned to SafeSteps competencies
- Evidence quality rubric
- Parent safety and stability checklist
- Support network and protective-capacity map
- Course-specific plans, such as regulation plans, parenting plans, budget plans, routines, digital agreements, and village maps

SafeSteps should only reference, track, or store outputs for:

- R-PAS
- MCMI-IV
- TSI-2
- PCA
- SASB
- AAI
- WAIS-IV
- Trail Making Test and Stroop when used as formal neuropsychological measures
- Conners Adult ADHD Rating Scale
- DPICS
- Emotional Availability Scales
- STEEP formal coding
- SDM when controlled by agency licence or policy
- FAF
- PBI
- PCRI
- CAT-P
- Forensic parenting capacity assessment protocols

For these external tools, SafeSteps should capture:

- Tool name and version
- Date administered
- Assessor name and qualification
- Platform or setting
- Score summary or interpretive summary
- Risk/protective implications
- Recommendations
- Uploaded report or evidence file
- Limits, caveats, and whether the result can be shared in court or child protection reporting

## Core Rubric Scale

Use a 1-4 anchored scale for SafeSteps program domains.

| Score | Label | Anchor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Not demonstrated | No reliable evidence of the capacity; concern remains high |
| 2 | Emerging | Some evidence, but inconsistent or dependent on significant support |
| 3 | Developing | Consistent in structured settings with some generalisation to real life |
| 4 | Established | Demonstrated independently across multiple contexts over time |

## Rubric Domains

### 1. Protective Capacity

Measures whether the parent can put the child's safety, wellbeing, and developmental needs ahead of adult pressure, conflict, or avoidance.

| Score | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Does not recognise the child's needs or minimises safety concerns |
| 2 | Can name some child needs when prompted but struggles to prioritise them under stress |
| 3 | Usually identifies child needs and takes protective steps in structured settings |
| 4 | Independently prioritises child safety and wellbeing across settings, including under pressure |

Evidence examples: safety plans, contact observations, child-focused reflections, support-network use.

### 2. Insight and Accountability

Measures understanding of why child protection concerns arose and whether the parent can take responsibility without blame-shifting or shame-based collapse.

| Score | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Denies, minimises, externalises, or blames others for the concerns |
| 2 | Acknowledges some concerns but remains defensive or inconsistent |
| 3 | Gives a coherent account of harm, impact, and personal responsibility |
| 4 | Shows sustained accountability, accepts feedback, repairs harm, and changes behaviour |

Evidence examples: reflective journals, facilitator notes, repair plans, feedback-response records.

### 3. Engagement and Motivation

Measures attendance, participation, homework completion, and whether engagement reflects genuine change rather than compliance only.

| Score | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Poor attendance, limited participation, or repeated non-completion |
| 2 | Attends inconsistently or completes tasks mainly when prompted |
| 3 | Attends consistently and completes most tasks with meaningful effort |
| 4 | Actively engages, seeks feedback, completes tasks, and applies learning outside sessions |

Evidence examples: attendance logs, homework records, course completion, facilitator review notes.

### 4. Emotional Regulation

Measures the parent's ability to notice escalation, use regulation strategies, and remain safe enough to parent under stress.

| Score | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Escalation, shutdown, aggression, or dysregulation remains unmanaged |
| 2 | Notices dysregulation after the fact and uses strategies only with strong support |
| 3 | Uses taught strategies in sessions and some real-life situations |
| 4 | Independently recognises early warning signs and regulates across multiple contexts |

Evidence examples: regulation plan, body-cue journal, contact observation, crisis-prevention plan.

### 5. Parenting Knowledge and Skills

Measures child-development understanding, safe interaction, routines, boundaries, repair, and practical parenting application.

| Score | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Limited understanding of child development or unsafe/inconsistent parenting responses |
| 2 | Can repeat concepts but applies them inconsistently |
| 3 | Applies parenting skills in structured or supported settings |
| 4 | Demonstrates safe, nurturing, developmentally appropriate parenting independently over time |

Evidence examples: lesson reflections, parent-child observation, routines, repair examples, worker notes.

### 6. Support Networks and Stability

Measures housing, service engagement, safe support people, practical routines, and capacity to ask for help before crisis.

| Score | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | No stable or safe support plan; key practical needs remain unmanaged |
| 2 | Some supports identified but plan is unclear or unreliable |
| 3 | Uses safe supports and services with some consistency |
| 4 | Maintains stable supports, routines, and service engagement with clear backup plans |

Evidence examples: support map, service appointment records, housing plan, financial/routine evidence.

### 7. Risk Factor Reduction

Measures change in known risk indicators such as substance use, family violence, unmanaged mental health, unsafe relationships, and crisis patterns.

| Score | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Risk indicators remain active, unmanaged, or denied |
| 2 | Some risk awareness exists but safety actions are inconsistent |
| 3 | Risk factors are actively managed with support and documented safety steps |
| 4 | Risk reduction is sustained, evidenced, and supported by protective routines and services |

Evidence examples: AUDIT/DAST-10 trajectory, mental health support plan, violence safety plan, service records.

## Longitudinal Tracking Points

Minimum recommended score points:

- Intake: Week 1 baseline
- Early review: Week 4 or first case review
- Midpoint: halfway through program
- Pre-contact review: before parent-child contact increases
- Exit: final assessment
- Follow-up: optional post-program review

## Scoring Guidance

- Score observable behaviour and evidence, not intention alone.
- Use the lowest defensible score when evidence is mixed and safety is uncertain.
- Record examples for every score, especially scores of 3 or 4.
- Distinguish parent self-report from worker observation, external records, and parent-child interaction evidence.
- Do not average away serious safety concerns. A high engagement score should not cancel out active risk.
- Use licensed instruments only according to their manuals, licences, and assessor qualification rules.

## Evidence Report Structure

1. Parent and child context
2. Assessment tools used and dates completed
3. Baseline profile
4. Program participation and attendance
5. Domain-by-domain rubric trajectory
6. Parent-child interaction observations
7. Risk indicators reduced or remaining
8. Protective capacities demonstrated
9. Evidence attachments reviewed
10. Professional recommendations
11. Limits of the report
