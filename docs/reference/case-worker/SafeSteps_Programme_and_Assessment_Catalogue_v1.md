# SafeSteps Programme and Assessment Catalogue

**Version:** 1.0  
**Vision owner:** Katrina Watts  
**Authoritative inputs:** SafeSteps Master Vision, Feature and Screen Register, and Complete User Journeys  
**Purpose:** Define the governed catalogue of programme pathways, course families, lesson standards, assessment phases, evidence levels and completion rules.

## 1. Catalogue principles

1. A programme is a structured change pathway, not a punishment or collection of videos.
2. Programme selection must consider whole-family assessment, child needs, stated concerns, required outcomes, available support, barriers, parent choice and professional review.
3. Duration and intensity may increase, decrease, pause or extend according to reviewed evidence and family circumstances.
4. No automated score may permanently label a family or independently assign a coercive programme.
5. Programme completion is separate from proof of parenting capacity, child safety and a legal decision.
6. Evidence quality, context and sustained application matter more than upload volume.
7. All required media uses live SafeSteps capture only and enters the Tamper-Proof Evidence Vault.
8. Programmes must support accessibility, trauma-informed pause/return, low-data use and full offline completion where required.
9. Missed work triggers barrier assessment and support before non-engagement can be considered.
10. Certificates state exactly what was completed and never claim reunification readiness or legal/clinical capacity.

## 2. Programme intensity levels

| ID | Level | Typical structure | Review | Evidence | Appropriate use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SS-INT-001 | Open self-directed | Optional lessons/activities; user-paced | User progress review | Optional or task-specific | Any parent seeking early support |
| SS-INT-002 | Guided | 3–5 learning activities weekly | Fortnightly or monthly | Selected practice evidence | Identified needs with moderate support |
| SS-INT-003 | Structured | 5–7 lessons/tasks weekly | Weekly summary and scheduled review | Multiple evidence sources | Significant defined concerns/outcomes |
| SS-INT-004 | Intensive | Daily 30–60 minute lesson and daily task/reflection | Weekly assessment plus reviewer/mentor check-in | Several weekly items including specified daily live photo/video | High-need preservation, reunification and specialist change pathways |
| SS-INT-005 | Maintenance | Reduced practice/check-ins after core pathway | Monthly/quarterly | Sustained-change samples | Reunification, case closure or relapse prevention |

Intensity is a service configuration, not a description of a person's worth. A qualified human review is required before intensity is used in a statutory or adverse context.

### Pathway adjustment rules

- The parent sees why a pathway is recommended, evidence used, expected workload, review date and alternatives.
- Parent may choose additional learning.
- A shorter or less intensive path may be approved when outcomes are demonstrated earlier.
- An extension requires recorded unmet outcomes, current evidence, support offered, barriers, parent response and reviewer rationale.
- A setback may change supports or tasks but does not erase prior progress.
- Provider or system failure cannot be counted as parent non-compliance.
- Changes are versioned and visible in the parent record.

## 4. Intensive programme weekly standard

### Daily requirements

- One structured 30–60 minute lesson.
- One practical task or reflection.
- Programme-specified live photo and/or video evidence where necessary and proportionate.
- Clear purpose for every evidence request.
- Save, pause and complete offline.
- Daily status shows saved locally, queued, syncing, failed or stored online.

### Weekly requirements

- Weekly assessment and progress summary.
- Several appropriately varied evidence sources.
- Scheduled worker, mentor or qualified reviewer check-in.
- Parent–child activity where appropriate and safe.
- Goals, barriers, support and next-week plan.
- Review of any clock, sync, integrity or evidence anomaly without automatic blame.

### Missed-work recovery

- Ask what prevented completion.
- Record technology, connectivity, disability, health, caring, service and other barriers.
- Preserve all completed work.
- Offer pause, extension, adjustment, rescheduling and catch-up plan.
- Notify a worker only under visible agreed rules or verified safety need.
- Record support offered before any non-engagement conclusion.

## 5. Standard lesson architecture

| Step | Stable lesson ID | Required component | Completion evidence |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | SS-LESS-001 | Definition and starting self-view | Saved response |
| 2 | SS-LESS-002 | Introduction, safety and outcomes | Opened/acknowledged |
| 3 | SS-LESS-003 | Captioned video or demonstration where helpful | Meaningful interaction, not watch time as capacity proof |
| 4 | SS-LESS-004 | Plain-language teaching and examples | Section completion |
| 5 | SS-LESS-005 | Understanding check | Valid response |
| 6 | SS-LESS-006 | Interactive activity | Durable activity result |
| 7 | SS-LESS-007 | Scenario practice | Reasoned response |
| 8 | SS-LESS-008 | Personal reflection | Saved reflection; unsafe disclosure not required |
| 9 | SS-LESS-009 | Real-life practice task | Task record |
| 10 | SS-LESS-010 | Action plan | Specific action, support and measure |
| 11 | SS-LESS-011 | Follow-up evidence | Required only when defined and proportionate |
| 12 | SS-LESS-012 | Formal assessment | Scored/qualified result |
| 13 | SS-LESS-013 | Quiz with critical-safety rules | Pass/retry history |
| 14 | SS-LESS-014 | End reflection | Comparison with starting view |
| 15 | SS-LESS-015 | Completion summary | Results, evidence, limits and next step |

Step 1 is mandatory for every lesson and must occur before any teaching is revealed. The parent answers what the lesson topic or definition means to them in their own words. No definition, example, hint, video, AI suggestion or preferred answer may be shown first. The exact baseline is preserved and compared with the mandatory end-definition reflection after learning. The baseline measures starting understanding; it is not a pass/fail item and cannot by itself support an adverse parenting-capacity conclusion.

## 7. Evidence ladder

Learning progress and competency evidence must be shown separately.

| Level | Evidence level | Meaning | Examples |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exposure | Content was accessed | Lesson opened, video controls used |
| 2 | Knowledge | Key information can be recalled | Quiz/knowledge check |
| 3 | Understanding | Person can explain meaning and relevance | Reflection, scenario rationale |
| 4 | Application | Skill is used in a real situation | Practice task, appropriately captured evidence |
| 5 | Behaviour | Observable behaviour reflects the skill | Visit/routine pattern, independent observation |
| 6 | Consistency | Behaviour is maintained across time/settings | Repeated evidence and reassessment |
| 7 | Mastery | Skill is adapted, sustained and recovered after setbacks | Longitudinal outcomes, repair and maintenance |

No single evidence type is sufficient for every level. AI coach conversations may support insight, engagement and pattern evidence but cannot independently establish behaviour, consistency, diagnosis or safety.

## 10. Assessment evidence-source model

Every material conclusion should identify which sources support it:

- direct observation;
- parent self-report and reflection;
- child voice with privacy safeguards;
- collateral information with authority and parent response;
- objective measures or records;
- programme and task evidence;
- visit patterns;
- service participation and provider outcomes;
- AI coach source-linked extracts and limitations;
- evidence-vault originals and verified derivatives;
- verified child-carer observations, with direct observation, child words and interpretation separated; and
- contradictory, missing or disputed information.

An assessment must not hide evidence that materially points in a different direction.

## 11. Assessment result standard

Every result must show:

1. tool name, version, purpose and applicable population;
2. assessor identity, qualifications and role;
3. date, phase and context;
4. evidence sources and exact vault references;
5. factors, scoring, weighting and method where used;
6. protective strengths as well as concerns;
7. missing, uncertain, disputed and contradictory information;
8. parent response and child safeguards;
9. limitations and what cannot be concluded;
10. recommended supports and reasonable adjustments;
11. reviewer sign-off; and
12. next assessment/review date.

## 12. Completion and certification

### Lesson completion

Required steps, valid responses, critical-safety rules and assigned evidence are complete. Accessibility adjustment may change method, not silently bypass the assessed outcome.

### Course completion

All required lessons, practice tasks, assessment rules and defined follow-up are complete.

### Programme completion

Programme-required courses and reviews are complete, but the report separately states demonstrated evidence levels, remaining needs and limitations.

### Certificate wording rule

A certificate may say that the user completed a named programme/course and its stated learning requirements. It must not say that the user is a safe parent, has proven parenting capacity, is ready for reunification, has no risk, meets a diagnosis, or should receive a particular legal outcome.

## 13. Programme governance and release gate

No programme or assessment may be presented as validated until it has:

- evidence and source review;
- qualified subject-matter review;
- lived-experience testing;
- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander cultural-safety review;
- accessibility and plain-language testing;
- child safeguarding review where applicable;
- legal, privacy and human-rights review;
- AI safety review where AI contributes;
- pilot use with measured outcomes;
- adverse-event and complaints process; and
- version history, review date and owner.
