# SafeSteps Program And Proof-Of-Evidence Framework

SafeSteps is an intensive reunification and parenting program designed to support safer family relationships while keeping the child voice central at every stage.

It combines:

- A parent-facing parenting and reunification curriculum.
- A child voice and safety participation model.
- Professional oversight and review workflows.
- Structured evidence capture.
- Progress, reflection, assessment, and reporting tools.

SafeSteps must be presented as structured support and decision-support documentation. It must not claim to guarantee reunification, custody outcomes, court outcomes, court approval, or statutory approval.

Recommended public wording:

SafeSteps is designed to support court-relevant documentation standards through structured evidence capture, child voice records, parenting progress tracking, assessment support, and professional review workflows.

Do not use "court approved" unless SafeSteps has written approval, endorsement, or recognition from a specific court, statutory body, regulator, or authorised program accreditation pathway.

## Program Identity

SafeSteps is not a simple course library. It is a reunification and parenting support system that connects learning, practice, reflection, evidence, assessment, and reporting.

The program is built around three voices:

| Voice | Purpose | Current launch position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parent voice | Learning, reflection, accountability, practical change, and evidence of parenting work | V1 launch focus |
| Child voice | Safety, feelings, trusted adults, boundaries, participation, and child-controlled sharing | Advanced module; needs safety review before full launch |
| Professional voice | Caseworker/facilitator review, risk reasoning, assessment, evidence review, and reporting | Advanced module; launch as decision-support only |

## Core Purpose

SafeSteps exists to help families and professionals:

- Prepare parents for safer family contact or reunification work.
- Strengthen protective parenting capacity.
- Capture the child voice respectfully and safely.
- Build a clear record of learning, practice, reflection, and evidence.
- Track progress over time instead of relying on a single snapshot.
- Identify gaps, risks, strengths, and demonstrated safety actions.
- Support professional judgement with structured records.

## Court-Relevant Documentation Standards

SafeSteps can say it is designed to support court-relevant documentation standards.

This means the system is structured to help produce records that are:

- Chronological.
- Factual and descriptive.
- Linked to evidence and attachments.
- Connected to parenting capacity, safety, stability, child voice, and professional review.
- Clear about what is observed, self-reported, uploaded, reviewed, unresolved, or contested.
- Built over time rather than relying on a single snapshot.
- Suitable for professional review and report preparation.

SafeSteps should not say "court approved standards" unless approval is obtained from an actual approving authority.

Preferred wording:

- Designed to support court-relevant documentation standards.
- Built for court-aware reunification and parenting documentation.
- Supports evidence-informed professional review.
- Produces structured records that can assist reports, case reviews, and legal documentation.

Avoid wording:

- Court approved.
- Court certified.
- Court guaranteed.
- Approved for reunification.
- Accepted by all courts.
- Forensic-grade unless audit, immutability, encryption, governance, and expert validation are complete.

## Four-Part Reunification Model

### Phase 1: Assessment And Stabilisation

Focus:

- Parent profile and needs.
- Child safety and wellbeing context.
- Household stability.
- Digital safety.
- Initial risks and protective factors.
- Safety planning.
- Baseline evidence and reflection records.

Expected outputs:

- Initial parent pathway.
- Assessment entry records.
- Safety and stability priorities.
- Evidence gaps.
- First tasks and learning path.

### Phase 2: Skill Building And Child Voice Integration

Focus:

- Parent curriculum.
- Area-of-need courses.
- Practical challenges.
- Reflections and meaning-making.
- Child voice participation where safe and authorised.
- Evidence of practice.

Expected outputs:

- Lesson completion records.
- Parent meaning reflections.
- Challenge/task records.
- Evidence uploads.
- Progress timeline entries.

### Phase 3: Reunification Readiness Review

Focus:

- Review of parent capacity indicators.
- Review of child wellbeing and safety indicators.
- Interaction quality where available and authorised.
- Risk reduction evidence.
- Protective capacity evidence.
- Unresolved evidence gaps.

Expected outputs:

- Readiness indicators.
- Risk review prompts.
- Protective factor summary.
- Evidence coverage summary.
- Professional review notes.

### Phase 4: Transition And Monitoring

Focus:

- Structured contact.
- Continued evidence capture.
- Safety plan compliance.
- Incident monitoring.
- Child voice updates.
- Ongoing professional review.

Expected outputs:

- Transition timeline.
- Contact and communication records.
- Incident and safety review notes.
- Final or periodic progress report.

## Parenting Program Structure

Parent-facing SafeSteps content should be organised as:

- Programs: longer pathways with month, week, day, reflection, task, evidence, and progress flow.
- Courses: standalone learning modules grouped around areas of need.
- Lessons: specific learning units that include parent meaning prompts.
- Challenges: practical actions that turn learning into behaviour.
- Reflections: structured insight records.
- Evidence: documents, media, notes, and practice records.

Every lesson should continue to include a parent meaning prompt so the parent is not only consuming content but connecting it to their own parenting.

## Child Voice Framework

Child voice in SafeSteps means more than asking for a child comment. It means the child’s safety, feelings, boundaries, trusted adults, and lived experience are treated as evidence that shapes the work.

Child voice can include:

- Feelings check-ins.
- Safety and trusted adult mapping.
- Child-friendly learning.
- Boundaries and body-safety tools.
- Child-controlled sharing.
- Story-based expression.
- Child progress and wellbeing records.

Launch constraint:

The child section should not be treated as production-ready until safety, privacy, sharing, and professional review boundaries are tested. Parent access to child-controlled material must remain mediated.

## Proof-Of-Evidence System

The proof-of-evidence system is the part of SafeSteps that turns activity into structured, reviewable records.

Its launch purpose is to support court-relevant documentation, not to make final legal, safety, or reunification findings.

Its purpose is to capture:

- What was done.
- When it happened.
- Who created the record.
- What evidence was attached.
- What parenting skill, safety action, reflection, or assessment it relates to.
- Whether it is a draft, stored, reviewed, shared, contested, or superseded.
- What remains unverified.

Evidence should be factual and descriptive. The app should not convert an uploaded item into a conclusion without review.

## Evidence Categories

SafeSteps evidence should support these categories:

| Category | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Learning evidence | Lesson completion, quiz/checkpoint records, parent meaning prompts |
| Reflection evidence | Parent reflections, growth notes, insight records |
| Practice evidence | Challenge completion, routine practice, communication practice |
| Document evidence | PDFs, letters, forms, certificates, professional documents |
| Media evidence | Photos, videos, live captures |
| Child voice evidence | Child-safe check-ins, trusted adults, boundaries, child-created records |
| Safety evidence | Safety plans, home safety checks, risk reduction actions |
| Stability evidence | Housing, routines, appointments, support network, financial stability |
| Professional evidence | Facilitator notes, caseworker review, assessment records, collateral reports |
| Incident evidence | Date, time, factual description, impact, response, follow-up, attachments |

## Six Evidence Engines

The attached brief describes the proof-of-evidence concept as multiple coordinated engines. In SafeSteps production terms, these should be implemented as follows:

### 1. Incident Evidence Engine

Captures factual incident records:

- Date and time.
- Location/context.
- People involved.
- What happened.
- Immediate impact on the child.
- Parent response.
- Professional response.
- Follow-up actions.
- Risk level.
- Attachments.

### 2. Pattern Recognition Engine

Looks across records for repeated or escalating patterns:

- Repeated unsafe behaviour.
- Repeated emotional triggers.
- Digital safety breaches.
- Inconsistent parent responses.
- Repeated missed tasks or gaps.
- Protective behaviour improving over time.

This should create prompts for review, not automated conclusions.

### 3. Protective Factor Engine

Tracks demonstrated strengths and protections:

- Safe routines.
- Emotional regulation.
- Child-focused communication.
- Stable support network.
- Practical care.
- Protective parenting behaviour.

Strengths must not be treated as proof of safety unless connected to demonstrated behaviour and evidence.

### 4. Child Voice Evidence Engine

Connects child voice records to safety and wellbeing review:

- Feelings and safety check-ins.
- Trusted adult mapping.
- Boundaries and worries.
- Child-controlled disclosures.
- Child-selected sharing.

Child-generated evidence should remain child-controlled unless explicit sharing rules allow otherwise.

### 5. Evidence Integrity Engine

Protects evidence records from casual alteration:

- Timestamp every record.
- Preserve original capture metadata where possible.
- Track file path and attachment metadata.
- Store status changes.
- Keep audit history for edits and reviews.
- Use offline vault hashes for queued evidence.

Current app status:

- The app has a first offline evidence vault with tamper-evident local hash-chain metadata.
- It queues failed uploads and syncs them when Supabase is reachable.
- This is tamper-evident, not yet a full forensic or encrypted evidence vault.

### 6. Report Evidence Engine

Builds reports from saved records:

- Progress summaries.
- Evidence coverage.
- Reflections.
- Tasks and challenges.
- Assessment records.
- Risk review prompts.
- Unresolved gaps.

Reports should distinguish:

- Observed facts.
- Parent self-report.
- Child voice records.
- Professional observations.
- Uploaded evidence.
- Automated prompts.
- Human review notes.

## Offline Evidence Vault Requirement

SafeSteps must support evidence capture when a parent is offline.

Minimum launch behavior:

- Parent can create an evidence record without internet.
- The record is queued locally.
- The queued record includes created time, attachment metadata, previous vault hash, and integrity hash.
- Pending records remain visible.
- The app retries upload when sync is triggered or the Evidence screen reloads.
- Synced records include offline capture time and vault hash in the notes.

Production hardening still required:

- Copy attachment files into app-controlled local storage.
- Use stronger cryptographic hashing.
- Consider encryption-at-rest for local pending evidence.
- Add automatic network-aware background sync.
- Add server-side immutable audit entries.
- Add a review state model such as draft, stored, reviewed, shared, contested, and superseded.

## Decision-Support Boundary

SafeSteps can support reunification work by producing structured evidence and review prompts.

SafeSteps must not claim:

- To make child protection decisions.
- To guarantee reunification.
- To replace caseworker, court, or clinical judgement.
- To make final safety findings automatically.
- To be court approved unless formal approval has actually been granted.

Recommended language:

- Readiness indicators.
- Risk review prompts.
- Evidence coverage.
- Professional judgement support.
- Structured progress record.
- Child voice summary.

Avoid language:

- Approved for reunification.
- Safe to reunify.
- Court-ready guarantee.
- Automated determination.
- Proven safe without review.

## Launch Implementation Priorities

1. Keep the parent-facing journey coherent.
2. Make course pathways and programs clear.
3. Make offline evidence reliable and visible.
4. Strengthen evidence metadata and statuses.
5. Connect evidence to reports without overclaiming.
6. Hold child/caseworker modules as advanced until privacy and safety are tested.
7. Add immutable server-side audit history before claiming forensic-grade evidence.
