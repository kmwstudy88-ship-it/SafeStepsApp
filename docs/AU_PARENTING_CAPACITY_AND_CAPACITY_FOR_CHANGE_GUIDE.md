# Australian Parenting Capacity and Capacity for Change Guide

## Purpose and boundary

SafeSteps may help authorised workers organise evidence for a suitably qualified professional. It must not present the AI, a score, or this workflow as a parenting-capacity assessment, psychological assessment, forensic opinion, child-protection decision, or legal conclusion.

The only automated outcome is **not ready** or **ready for qualified review**. SafeSteps must never label a person fit/unfit, safe/unsafe, credible/not credible, or likely/unlikely to receive a particular court or agency outcome.

Australian requirements vary by jurisdiction, role, service, proceeding, and information-sharing authority. MARAM is a Victorian framework; it must not be represented as the national default. The applicable state or territory framework and organisational obligations must be confirmed before use.

## Evidence model

Parenting capacity is child-specific and contextual. A defensible professional formulation should integrate, where lawful and relevant:

- the child's development, health, disability, culture, relationships, trauma experience, wishes, and current needs;
- direct parent-child observation across relevant settings and over time;
- caregiver history, present functioning, stressors, strengths, insight, and protective behaviour;
- authorised collateral information and service records;
- family-violence, coercive-control, alcohol-and-other-drug, mental-health, and environmental information;
- evidence of actual change outside a program setting; and
- uncertainty, conflicting evidence, limitations, and reasonable alternative explanations.

No diagnosis, screening result, personality measure, attendance record, self-report, or single observation proves parenting capacity. Cultural difference, disability, distress, poverty, housing instability, or service access barriers must not be treated as incapacity without a relevant, evidence-based connection to the child's needs and safety.

## Capacity for change

Qualified review should consider:

1. **Insight and accountability** — understanding the child's experience and the effect of behaviour without relying on rehearsed language alone.
2. **Motivation and engagement** — willingness to participate, accept appropriate help, and work toward child-centred goals.
3. **Demonstrated behavioural change** — observable application of safer parenting and regulation skills in real situations.
4. **Sustainability and generalisation** — change maintained across time, stress, settings, and setbacks, including a realistic relapse or safety plan.
5. **Protective capacity** — ability and willingness to recognise risk, prioritise the child, seek help, and act protectively.

Program completion is evidence of participation, not proof of change. Conversely, barriers to attendance should be investigated rather than automatically interpreted as lack of motivation.

## Assessment-method catalogue

SafeSteps may reference high-level metadata for methods such as MIM, PSI-4, AAPI-2.1, HOME, AUDIT, DUDIT, SASSI-4, SARA, ODARA, MMPI-3, PAI, DASS-21, CBCL, ASQ-3, and TSCC. Inclusion in the catalogue is not endorsement for a particular case or Australian jurisdiction.

Before any method is used, the service must verify:

- current publisher or owner permissions and licence terms;
- required professional qualifications, accreditation, and training;
- population, language, cultural, age, disability, and jurisdictional suitability;
- the current authorised version and scoring process;
- consent or other lawful authority, collection notice, purpose, access, sharing, retention, and deletion controls; and
- how limitations and uncertainty will be documented.

SafeSteps must not reproduce proprietary questions, stimulus material, manuals, scoring keys, norms, or automated interpretations unless specific written authorisation and governance approval exist. The safer default is to store external-report metadata, an authorised summary, assessor identity and qualification, date/version, limitations, and review status.

## Mandatory human review gates

A report is not ready for qualified review when any of the following applies:

- no suitably qualified assessor is identified;
- child-specific needs are absent;
- the conclusion relies on one source, a diagnosis, or a test score;
- direct observation, authorised collateral, demonstrated change, sustainability, or protective capacity is missing;
- a restricted method's licence, training, or administration requirements are unconfirmed;
- family violence is relevant but the applicable jurisdictional framework is unconfirmed; or
- the content attempts a legal finding, predicts an agency/court outcome, or labels a parent fit/unfit.

Safeguarding, privacy, information-sharing, mandatory-reporting, and court-use workflows require written approval from appropriately qualified Australian clinical, safeguarding, privacy, and legal reviewers before production use.
