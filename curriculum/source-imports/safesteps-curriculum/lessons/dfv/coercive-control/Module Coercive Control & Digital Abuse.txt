1. Defining Coercive ControlCoercive control is a strategic pattern of behavior used by an abuser to dominate, isolate, and restrict a partner’s personal freedom and autonomy over time. Unlike overt physical violence, coercive control often operates subtly in plain sight.                    +-----------------------------+

Core ComponentsIsolation: Deliberately severing contact with family, friends, colleagues, or support networks to ensure total dependency.Micro-regulation: Dictating daily choices, such as what to wear, when to sleep, what to eat, or how to perform household tasks.Financial Subjugation: Restricting access to bank accounts, limiting employment opportunities, or creating forced debt.Psychological Degradation: Systematically eroding self-esteem through constant criticism, body-shaming, or gaslighting.2. Digital Abuse & Tech-Facilitated CoercionAs technology evolves, coercive control increasingly manifests in digital spaces. Abusers leverage everyday devices and software to enforce monitoring and compliance.Common Digital Abuse TacticsTacticDescriptionExampleLocation SurveillanceMisusing GPS tools or tracking tags without informed consent.Placing an AirTag or SmartTag in a purse, car, or coat; abusing "Find My" features.Account HijackingDemanding passwords or setting up dual-factor alerts on personal accounts.Forcing a partner to share iCloud, Google, or social media credentials.Stalkerware / SpywareSecretly installing software to record activity, ambient audio, or keystrokes.Installing hidden monitoring apps to view text messages and call logs remotely.Digital PunishmentThreatening or executing digital exposure or access deprivation.Remotely locking smart home devices, changing Wi-Fi passwords, or threatening image dissemination.3. Interactive Scenario: Identifying Digital Red FlagsScenario:Sam gives Taylor a new phone as a gift, pre-configured with all of Taylor's favorite apps. Shortly after, Taylor notices that whenever they make plans with a friend, Sam sends a text within minutes asking, "Why are you at that café?"When Taylor asks about it, Sam says, "I just worry about your safety, so I enabled location sharing. If you have nothing to hide, it shouldn't be an issue."Discussion & AnalysisRed Flag: Pre-configuring the phone allowed Sam to set up account access and location-sharing without Taylor's active consent.Manipulative Tactic: Framing surveillance as "worrying about safety" and weaponizing transparency ("if you have nothing to hide").Impact: Taylor experiences hyper-vigilance and begins self-isolating to avoid confrontation.4. Digital Safety & Risk Mitigation StrategiesCrucial Safety Note: Removing tracking software or changing passwords immediately can alert an abuser and escalate danger. Always prioritize personal safety and consult a trained domestic violence advocate before making sudden technical changes.Recommended Safety ProtocolUse a Safe Device: Perform research, contact support, or plan exit strategies from a safe computer or phone (e.g., at a library, trusted friend's house, or a workplace device).Audit Connected Devices: Regularly check connected devices on Google, Apple, or social media account security settings.Securing Location Settings: Review app-level location permissions (e.g., Maps, social networks, messaging apps) on personal devices.Documenting Evidence: Take screenshots or log dates, times, and specific incidents if legal documentation or restraining orders are needed.5.
